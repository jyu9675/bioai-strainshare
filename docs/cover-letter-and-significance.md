## Significance blurb (short, reusable in abstracts & summaries)

> Rectal-to-vaginal transmission of pathogens is textbook obstetrics — it is why every pregnancy includes
> a recto-vaginal GBS swab — and it is supported by converging evidence: the same GBS genotype in the
> rectum and vagina of 18 of 19 colonized women, rectal carriage as the strongest predictor of vaginal
> colonization, and re-seeding of the vagina from the bowel after antibiotics. But this evidence is
> entirely cross-sectional and culture-based. This study delivers the missing piece — the same strain
> tracked *in situ*, from metagenomes, across the whole community, over the course of pregnancy — and is
> the first to test whether limited water, sanitation, hygiene, and antenatal care make that transfer
> more likely. Because it targets the organisms that already have prevention levers (intrapartum
> prophylaxis, WASH, microbiome-directed biotherapeutics), its results are directly actionable for the
> populations that bear the greatest burden.

## Funder-fit notes

- **NIH — NICHD** (pregnancy, preterm birth, maternal-child health) or **NIAID** (host-microbe, GBS/
  *E. coli*): lead with preterm birth + neonatal infection and the strain-resolved mechanism; R01 for the
  full cohort, R21 for the pilot.
- **Gates Foundation** (maternal, newborn & child health; LMIC): lead with the **equity/WASH** angle and
  actionable prevention in low-resource settings; the 24.1% LMIC GBS burden and preventability are the hook.
- **Wellcome Trust** (infection, global health): the discovery-to-prevention arc and open tooling fit well.
- **GAPPS / preterm-birth consortia**: the cohort-partnership and comparator (MOMS-PI) framing.
