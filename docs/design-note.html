Design Note · Tool + Complementary Research

A standardized tool for gut ↔ vaginal bacterial strain transmission

Two deliverables from one codebase: (1) a portable transmission tracker that turns paired gut and vaginal metagenomes into shared-strain and direction calls, and (2) a “VALENCIA-for-strains” standard — fixed thresholds and a built-in null so calls are comparable across studies. Both grow directly out of your existing BIOAI pilot.

For: Kwon Lab collaboration angle Basis: BIOAI strain pilot + 22 verified findings Date: 2026-08-31

The one-paragraph thesis. The field has independently produced the two ingredients this tool needs but never joined them. On the clinical side, vaginal transplantation and live biotherapeutics already require strain-resolved tracking to work — donor L. crispatus strains are followed into recipients, and a woman’s own endogenous strains form a competing reservoir that resists displacement. On the methods side, strain-sharing thresholds are mature but unstandardized, and no existing tool infers transmission direction or survives the low biomass of vaginal samples. Nobody has asked whether the gut is the reservoir seeding the vagina. That question is the whitespace — and it maps cleanly onto cohorts the Kwon Lab already holds.

I

What the evidence actually says

Graded from a fan-out literature pass (21 sources → 98 claims → 25 adversarially verified, 22 confirmed). The grade tells you how hard you can lean on each fact when writing a grant or a paper.

Established

Vaginal transplant & live-biotherapeutic engraftment is already tracked at strain level

VMT pilot (NCT04046900): 3/4 recipients shifted to L. crispatus dominance at 1 month (durable ≥6 mo in 2), 0/placebo — with donor-derived strains directly traced in. LACTIN-V/CTV-05 used StrainFacts to separate the administered strain from endogenous ones (30% vs 9% dominance at wk12; benefit ratio 3.4, CI 1.4–8.1). Species-level readouts are provably insufficient.

medRxiv 2025.08.27.25334544 · Cell Host & Microbe S1931-3128(26)00090-9

Established

An endogenous strain reservoir governs whether therapy takes

Among 18 visits with high native-strain abundance, 61% retained it and none flipped to the administered CTV-05; native strains displaced the drug but were almost never displaced by it. Colonization success was predictable from pre-treatment state (bacterial load, diversity, pH, baseline IL-1β).

Cell Host & Microbe S1931-3128(26)00090-9

Established

Shared strains ≠ transmission, and never imply direction

Shared environment and diet inflate sharing independent of any transmission; the same signal reflects persistence just as well as transfer. The stringent thresholds that exclude ~95% of false sharing also discard ~91% of true events. TRACS (2026) states outright it “does not determine transmission direction.” Directionality must come from longitudinal acquisition timing, not from a sharing call.

Microbiome s40168-025-02051-8 · Nat Microbiol s41564-026-02339-x · inStrain docs

Established

The thresholds are concrete — but assume coverage vaginal samples often lack

inStrain: popANI ≥ 99.999% + ≥50% genome compared; ~50% breadth needs ~1× but full breadth needs >6×, and detection needs thousands of mapped reads — self-declared “less ideal for low depth.” StrainGE detects sharing down to 0.5× (markers cover ~74% of genome vs StrainPhlAn’s ~1.4%), but its assembly step degrades on low-biomass input. No single tool cleanly covers the vaginal case.

inStrain docs · Genome Biology s13059-022-02630-0

Emerging / Open

The gut-as-reservoir hypothesis is untested — this is the whitespace

The competing reservoir demonstrated so far is endogenous vaginal strains. Whether a gut Lactobacillus (or Gardnerella/Prevotella/E. coli/enterococcal) reservoir seeds the vagina and predicts engraftment is unexamined. Recent vaginal strain work analysed 354 vaginal metagenomes and zero gut samples. L. crispatus is more strain-diverse than L. iners — the better cross-site tracer.

npj Biofilms s41522-025-00682-1 · Cell Host & Microbe S1931-3128(26)00090-9

Emerging / Open

HIV/ART reshape the gut in a geography-dependent way — cross-site effect unknown

Ragon/Broad metagenomics of 327 people with HIV + 260 controls (Uganda, Botswana, USA) found HIV-associated shifts are mostly site-specific. Whether HIV/ART or geography changes gut→FGT translocation is a novel extension over cohorts the lab already has.

Nat Microbiol s41564-025-02157-7

Do not build on

“SNPs prove vagina→uterus directional translocation” — refuted (0–3 / 1–2)

Two attempts to source directional cross-site SNP evidence from the 2021 Nat Commun uterus/vagina paper failed verification. Do not cite cross-site directionality as established. Likewise the most optimistic StrainGE figure (0.1× / <0.1% abundance) was refuted (0–3) — 0.5× is the defensible low-coverage bound.

refuted: Nat Commun s41467-021-24516-8 · Genome Biology s13059-022-02630-0

II

What the tool is — and what it must detect

Your BIOAI pipeline already implements the hard middle. The tool wraps it into something portable and standardized, then adds the three things the evidence in Part I says are missing: a low-biomass path, honest direction handling, and confound filters.

Input A Gut metagenome stool / rectal shotgun. The candidate reservoir.
→
Core engine · you have this profile → compare inStrain popANI, dual-reported at 0.999 and canonical 0.99999; breadth ≥ 0.5; within-vs-between-person null built in.
→
Input B Vaginal metagenome low-biomass. The recipient site.
M1

Shared-strain call have · BIOAI

The locked-threshold popANI call and the within-vs-between-person null — your existing 05_shared_strain_analysis.py. This is the rigor move most papers skip; it stays the core.

M2

Contamination discriminator have · BIOAI

Shared strain + dissimilar community (Bray–Curtis) = translocation; + similar community = cross-swab contamination. Add same-plate / CroCoDeEL flags when plate layout exists.

M3

Low-biomass fallback path build

When a vaginal sample fails inStrain’s coverage floor, route the target through StrainGE (down to 0.5×). Directly answers inStrain’s self-declared low-depth weakness — the single biggest failure mode for FGT samples.

M4

Direction inference — honest by construction build

Never infer direction from a sharing call. With longitudinal timepoints, call direction from acquisition timing (gut-before-vagina vs reverse). Cross-sectional pairs are reported as “shared, direction-unresolved” — not guessed.

M5

Generalist-strain filter build

Down-weight strains shared across unrelated people (the confound Part I flags). Focus calls on recently-acquired, person-specific strains to cut false sharing.

M6

The standard: fixed thresholds + portable config build · this is #4

Package M1–M5 as a versioned Snakemake/Nextflow workflow with fixed, reference-based thresholds and a citable spec — exactly VALENCIA’s move from per-study clustering to comparable calls. Precedent exists: SameStr already ships defaults (MVS ≥99.9% over ≥5 kb).

Why this is credible, not speculative: M1–M2 are running code today. M3–M6 are integration and packaging of published methods, not new algorithms. The novelty is the standard and the application, which is exactly why VALENCIA — also “just” a nearest-centroid classifier — became field infrastructure.

III

Complementary research the tool unlocks

Four studies, each anchored to a specific Kwon Lab paper and a cohort type the lab already works in. The plum cell is what the tool detects that nothing else currently can.

Study A · flagship

Does a gut Lactobacillus reservoir predict engraftment?

Extends

The CTV-05 “competing reservoir” finding (Cell Host & Microbe 2026).

Cohort

VMT / LACTIN-V recipients with paired stool at baseline + follow-up.

Tool detects

Whether recipients carrying a gut L. crispatus strain engraft better/worse — and if the engrafting strain is donor, vaginal-endogenous, or gut-seeded.

Study B

BV recurrence: strain persistence vs re-seeding

Extends

Metronidazole → L. iners dominance & cystine-uptake inhibition (Nat Microbiol 2022).

Cohort

Longitudinal post-treatment BV, weekly vaginal + gut.

Tool detects

Is relapsing L. iners the same persisting strain, a re-seeded strain from the gut, or newly acquired? Only longitudinal strain identity can separate these.

Study C

HIV/ART × geography and cross-site translocation

Extends

Geography-dependent HIV gut effects (Nat Microbiol 2025).

Cohort

Existing South Africa / Uganda / Zimbabwe / Botswana cohorts; add paired FGT sampling.

Tool detects

Whether HIV/ART or region shifts the rate of gut→FGT strain sharing — a mucosal-barrier readout tied to the lab’s translocation work.

Study D · the methods paper

The “VALENCIA-for-strains” standard itself

Extends

VALENCIA’s standardization logic (Microbiome 2020).

Cohort

Your Goltsman/DiGiulio pilot (PRJNA288562) + synthetic ground truth + the 191-sample V–R set.

Tool detects

Benchmarks fixed thresholds, the within/between null, contamination + low-biomass handling into one citable spec. This is the paper that makes A–C comparable and is closest to shippable now.

Sequencing suggestion. Ship D first — it’s your pilot promoted to a methods paper and it earns the tool its citation. Then A is the highest-impact biology and the most direct extension of the lab’s newest flagship. B and C follow as the standard proves out.

IV

Risks the tool must handle up front

These are the failure modes reviewers will raise. Each already has a mitigation in the module map — stating them plainly is what separates a standard from a script.

RiskWhy it bites hereMitigation
Direction is unknowable from sharing Every existing tool (inStrain, StrainPhlAn, StrainGE, TRACS) refuses to call direction. M4 — direction only from longitudinal timing; cross-sectional flagged unresolved.
Low-biomass vaginal samples fail coverage inStrain needs >6× for full breadth; many FGT samples never reach it. M3 — StrainGE fallback to 0.5× (0.1× claim is refuted; don’t promise it).
Sensitivity/specificity cliff Stringent cutoffs drop ~91% of true sharing; loose ones admit environmental look-alikes. Dual-report both thresholds + M1 within/between null as the real significance test.
Shared-environment confound Diet/cohabitation inflate sharing with no transmission at all. M5 generalist-strain filter + M2 community-dissimilarity check.
Cross-swab contamination masquerades as translocation A whole-community “soup” transfer looks identical to one strain moving. M2 — translocation = shared strain + dissimilar community; else flag.
